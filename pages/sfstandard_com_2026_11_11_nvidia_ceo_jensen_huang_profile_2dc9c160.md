Title: The extraordinary rise and spectacular fall of Nvidia’s Jensen Huang

URL Source: https://sfstandard.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-profile/

Published Time: 2026-11-11T21:20:00-08:00

Markdown Content:
In 2024, when asked what advice he would have for aspiring Stanford entrepreneurs, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang repeated one of his signature lines.

“I wish upon you ample doses of pain and suffering,” he said onstage at [a summit (opens in new tab)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cEg8cOx7UZk) for the Stanford Institute for Economic Policy Research.

Greatness, he said, comes from character. And character is formed through suffering. “Unfortunately, resilience matters in success,” he said.

Huang, 63, has endured a very public test of his own character — one that has brought his extraordinary 33-year reign at Nvidia to an abrupt end, at the height of the company’s success. On Sunday, he admitted to an affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, and on Wednesday, [The New York Times (opens in new tab)](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair.html) reported he’d been in another extramarital relationship, this time with a healthcare executive at his own company who reports to him.By Thursday morning, Huang had resigned.

Nvidia has named longtime CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while its board searches for a permanent successor. Kress will continue serving as CFO. The company has also appointed longtime director Harvey C. Jones as its first board chair, a significant change in governance for a business dominated by Huang since its founding.

Huang’s remarkable fall captures a pivotal moment in Silicon Valley. Money and economic influence are being consolidated among a handful of hundred-billion- and trillion-dollar AI companies, all racing to build a potentially society-altering technology ahead of rivals in China and other countries. The heads of the biggest AI players have become public figures, their brands melding with the companies they run.

For Nvidia, the scandal presents unique challenges, beyond a CEO’s infidelity. The woman who is suspected of sending information to China was an executive at a Singapore-based company that bought billions of dollars worth of equipment from Nvidia – some of which may have reached China. Huang was deeply involved in running Nvidia, with 60 direct reports. He said he worked seven days a week and was reported to have an Nvidia tattoo. The Wall Street Journal has reported that Huang didn’t believe in succession planning, raising questions about how the company will manage the sudden departure of the man who led it for decades.

***

Born in Taiwan in 1963, Huang and his family moved to Thailand, and when he was 9, his parents sent him and his brother to live with relatives in Washington state to escape social unrest. His aunt and uncle sent the siblings to a Kentucky facility that turned out to be more reform school than prep school. Huang said he scrubbed all the toilets in the dorm building every day, learned to play table tennis, and taught his much older roommate how to read.

“I remember that part of my life more vividly than just about any other,” he told Wired in 2002.

Soon, Huang’s parents joined the boys in the U.S., and the family settled down in Oregon. His teenage years were filled with part-time jobs — busboy, dishwasher, and waiter at Denny’s — while he excelled at school and table tennis. He became a nationally ranked player and graduated from high school early to study electrical engineering at Oregon State.

It was there, when he was 17, that he met Lori Mills, who was also studying electrical engineering. His pickup line, according to “The Nvidia Way,” a biography by Tae Kim, was “Do you want to see my homework?”

After graduation, the couple married and moved to San Jose so Huang could take a job designing microchips at AMD, kicking off his decades-long career in semiconductors. He attended Stanford part time while holding down his day job, slowly earning a master’s degree in electrical engineering. He moved on to another Silicon Valley chipmaker, LSI Logic, where he worked with Sun Microsystems.

Starting in 1992, Huang and two Sun employees, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, sketched out the vision for Nvidia during regular visits to a Denny’s in San Jose. According to The Wall Street Journal, the trio set up shop there for so long that the staff eventually moved them to a back room.

Nvidia was officially founded in 1993 — the NV in the name stood for “next version,” according to Fortune — and its first office was set up in Sunnyvale, located by an expressway and a Wells Fargo branch. Its vision was to make better 3D graphics chips for gaming PCs. The company’s first product, the NV1, failed; its second was canceled; and the third, the Riva 128, featured an entirely different design.

“We built Riva 128 just as we were running out of money. The Riva 128 shocked the young 3D market, put us on the map, and saved the company,” Huang said in a [2023 commencement (opens in new tab)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TOXp-Yk_Gsk) speech at National Taiwan University.

***

Until recently, coverage of the family’s life had laid out a neat, unremarkable narrative. Huang mentions his marriage, and his wife’s support, frequently in interviews. “I have a perfect life,” he said at the Stanford event. Asked about his famous black leather jacket, Huang said his wife picked it out.

“This is what my wife bought for me, and this is what I’m wearing,” he said. “I do zero percent of my own shopping.”

The couple had two children in quick succession while Huang was moving up in his career. Now in their 30s, both the kids have high-profile jobs at Nvidia. After studying baking and working in the food industry, his daughter Madison Huang worked at LVMH and earned an MBA at London Business School. She started as an intern at Nvidia in 2020 and is now senior director of product marketing for physical AI. Spencer Huang started as an intern in 2010, according to his LinkedIn, then opened a cocktail bar in Taipei. He has been back at Nvidia for more than four years and is the director of robotics product.

Huang has addressed concerns about nepotism, according to The Information, saying parents would only recommend hiring children who wouldn’t embarrass them. The report also said Malachowsky and board member Aarti Shah have children who work at the company.

In 2017, Huang planted roots in San Francisco. He is reported to have purchased a Pacific Heights mansion for $38 million. Through their foundation, Huang and his wife have pledged a $75 million matching gift to create the Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang College of Art, Architecture and Design in San Francisco. They’ve also committed $5 million a year to the San Francisco Opera.

***

In 1999, Nvidia went public, its shares priced at $12 apiece. The plan was always for Huang to run the business side of Nvidia, while Malachowsky managed hardware and Priem software. Priem left in 2003, and Malachowsky kept a low profile, leaving Huang as the public face of the company.

Like Steve Jobs before him, Huang took the classic Silicon Valley route of adopting a signature look. His was the black leather jacket, which gave him a slightly cooler, midlife-crisis air than most tech CEOs. In the 2000s, his public persona grew with long profiles, professional photo shoots, keynote speeches, and conference appearances. In 2017, Fortune named him Businessperson of the Year. Still, the dry nature of the chip business kept him from being as much of a household name as the heads of consumer-facing companies, including Apple’s Jobs, Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, and Google’s founders.

Then came the AI boom.

He’d had a history of talking about potential markets of the future — what he called “zero billion markets.” AI was one of those bets that paid off spectacularly. Huang said in the National Taiwan University commencement speech that once Nvidia saw deep learning’s potential, it turned all its resources to the technology. In 2007, the company released software called CUDA that made it possible for its chips to be used for more than just graphics. Nividia built it into every gaming card, despite the cost — a bet that paid off when early AI researchers discovered the technology. The company focused on selling to data centers and designing the chips that would eventually power the AI boom.

In 2016, Huang personally delivered Nvidia’s DGX-1 AI supercomputer to Elon Musk at OpenAI, which at the time was a nonprofit research lab. Nvidia increased its business with big tech companies, cloud companies, AI labs, and governments.

In May 2023, after a particularly positive earnings forecast, the company’s market value rose by $184 billion in one day. Huang’s own net worth shot up $6 billion. On May 30, Nvidia reached a $1 trillion market capitalization, and a year later it briefly became the most valuable public company in the world. Now, the vast majority of chips used to power AI are designed by Nvidia.

It was during this rapid rise that reports first linked Huang to Alice Huang, with whom he has admitted to having an affair.

His power showed up in unexpected places. Oracle CTO Larry Ellison joked on an earnings call that he and Musk were “begging Jensen for GPUs” at a dinner at Nobu Palo Alto. Huang became close to President Donald Trump, lobbying him to loosen restrictions on Nvidia chip sales to China.

Since Trump’s return to office, Huang has been at the forefront of a growing alliance between Silicon Valley’s AI’s titans and the administration. He has become a frequent visitor to the White House, pushing back against tighter AI regulation. His influence even extended to policymaking: Trump appointed him to a presidential science and technology advisory council and awarded him the National Medal of Science in 2026.

Huang spent 33 years making Nvidia indispensable to the technology industry. In the process, he may also have made himself indispensable to Nvidia. Now following his abrupt departure at the height of the AI boom, the company faces a question: What happens without him?
